Part I. God, creation, and the restoration of stewardship

Chapter 13. The material world and the Gnostic question

We take seriously the question of why creation feels broken. We do not answer by declaring matter a prison, the body a disgrace, or ordinary life a mistake from which only possessors of secret knowledge may escape.

The Gnostic Resolution enters a framework it does not claim as established fact. It asks: if a limited maker such as Yaldabaoth stood between us and the ultimate source, would hatred and overthrow heal that relation? Its answer is love without submission to ignorance. We receive this as a discipline of conditional theological reasoning. We do not quietly turn the hypothetical Demiurge into a required article of belief.

The distinction it supplies is essential. Ignorance is a condition; willing evil is a posture. Understanding a harmful limitation may change the way we attempt correction. It does not require permitting the harm to continue. Loving a limited being means working for that being's truthful participation in a larger good, not confirming its claim to be the whole. We may therefore love an official, parent, teacher, or founder while ending the authority through which that person harms others.

The sparks are not loot to be extracted from creation. We receive the mandate to let life, consciousness, love, and order find fuller expression, rather than compress the world's worth back into one possessive center. Trees, animals, soils, waters, and mountains are not spiritually valueless because they do not speak our language. Our theism deepens their claim upon our attention.

The source leaves two possible ends: a completed creation returning to fullness, or a completed layer becoming the foundation for another. We do not make either a timetable. Both direct our present practice toward care rather than escape. A religion faithful to this resolution will be found feeding, learning, restoring, and keeping promises. It will not wait for the world's disappearance before deciding whether the world deserves love.
