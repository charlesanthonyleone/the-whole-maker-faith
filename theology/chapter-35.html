Part III. Worship, household, and the practices of a living faith

Chapter 35. Animals, ecosystems, and the circulation of care

We receive nonhuman life as a participant in creation, not spiritual scenery for the human drama. Our capacity to name, measure, breed, move, or kill an organism does not make its existence meaningless apart from our wishes. Stewardship begins by learning what a living system requires on its own terms.

The circulation described in God's Math teaches us to ask where food, water, materials, energy, and waste actually go. It deepens gratitude for the many lives that make a meal possible and responsibility for what a household returns to the world. It does not establish that every natural event is morally exemplary. Predation occurs; it does not license a human being to organize society as predation.

We do not require one universal diet as a test of holiness. We do require serious attention to avoidable cruelty, ecological cost, nutrition, cultural circumstance, and the means by which food is obtained. Traditions of fasting or abstention must not endanger health. A member who depends on an affordable food must not be publicly shamed by someone whose apparent purity is supported by resources unavailable to them.

Our communities should undertake forms of care they can sustain: reducing waste, repairing rather than replacing where sound, tending soil and water, learning local species, supporting responsible cultivation, or restoring damaged habitat. We judge a project by what survives after its inaugural photograph. Planting is not the whole duty; the planted thing must be tended.

The animal-before-the-traveler principle governs our relation to dependent creatures. We prepare for their needs before using them in work, recreation, or ceremony. No animal is to be harmed in an attempt to demonstrate spiritual power, appease an adversary, or dramatize a narrative. We establish no required animal sacrifice.

We also acknowledge limits. Conservation of material does not make pollution harmless, and a hope for future recirculation does not justify present poisoning. The source's proposal that dark matter is dead energy in transit is speculative, not established cosmology. It must not be invoked to give religious certainty to an environmental or engineering claim. Reverence requires better attention to real consequences, not confidence that the universe will repair whatever we choose not to count.
