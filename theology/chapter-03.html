Part I. God, creation, and the restoration of stewardship

Chapter 3. Reason, mathematics, and the address of prayer

We receive reason as a sacred capacity. To ask how a thing holds together is not to profane it. To discover a relation that can be expressed mathematically is not to remove God from it. We believe the intelligibility of reality is one of the ways its depth becomes available to us. The person who does careful reasoning may be serving truth more faithfully than the person who decorates an unsupported assertion with a divine title.

An atheist may recognize the bond between the bricks as mathematics: the intelligible order by which relationships become functioning wholes. We can work, study, and care together on that recognition. We need not insist that every collaborator secretly believes what we believe. Agreement about a relation does not settle agreement about its ultimate source. Our religion addresses that source as God; our public cooperation does not require our neighbor to do so.

We therefore refuse two evasions. We will not rename God as an impersonal abstraction merely to conceal that we are a theistic faith. We will not demand that everyone who understands order profess our theology as the price of receiving food, education, protection, friendship, or civic standing. The skeptical participant is not a broken believer awaiting correction by force. Honest doubt is a possible form of refusing false gods.

Mathematical rigor is also a limit on religious language. A valid equation does not, by itself, establish the existence of the spiritual entities to which a writer compares it. Conservation does not demonstrate personal immortality. Emergence does not prove providential intention. Numerical patterns in religious stories do not establish a shared prehistoric event. We may find these patterns theologically illuminating without giving them evidentiary powers they do not possess.

Prayer and reason meet in disciplined attention. We ask what is true, what we may have missed, what our desire is protecting, and what a faithful next act would require. We pray for correction as earnestly as for confirmation. A religion that fears a correct calculation has mistaken its own account for the Whole Maker's ledger.
