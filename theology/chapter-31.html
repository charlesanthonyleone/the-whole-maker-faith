Part III. Worship, household, and the practices of a living faith

Chapter 31. Household, complementarity, and the covenant that tells the truth

We receive the household as an early school of communion: a place where desire, difference, care, resources, and time must become more than competing claims. We teach complementarity as design rather than domination. Distinct capacities can form a whole without either person becoming the other's derivative or instrument.

The Solar Household pictures generative direction as sun, attuned mediation as moon, and children as planets with distinct orbits. Voluntary Submission describes discerning reception that calls forth devotion, not an entitlement to compliance. Polarity Suppression warns against requiring one person to sustain every function alone. We receive their call to distributed care and trustworthy difference. We retain their proposed and contested status; their clinical predictions are not established diagnoses, and their strongly sexed claims are not an infallible map of every household.

The source sometimes describes masculine and feminine as functional principles not equivalent to men and women; elsewhere it assigns them sharply by sex. We leave this tension visible. We will not enforce the image by denying a woman's directional capacity, a man's tenderness, or a household's need to distribute work according to its actual members. No ministry or community office is restricted by sex. No one's orientation or gender identity excuses the community from the obligations of dignity and truthful encounter.

We honor the Natural Covenant's Explorer, Bound, Crowned, and Hearth Seal distinctions as attempts to replace counterfeit commitment with honest promises. We do not erase the disagreement within them. Crowned is first associated with chastity and fidelity, then with a stable household open to outside sexual experience. Hearth Seal is described both as a lifelong uninterrupted pair and as an attainment available after earlier relationships. Until those definitions are resolved, a community must state the actual promise rather than grant a title whose witnesses mean different things by it.

We honor sustained fidelity without requiring every adult to enter a sexual or reproductive household. Celibate, single, widowed, and nonromantic shared lives can be faithful. Plural adult covenants described in the source require the same honesty, freedom, safety, and care for dependents as any other; a spiritual label supplies no exemption from law or from unequal power.

The household's greatness is measured by whether its people can become more truthful and alive within it. It is not measured by how effectively it prevents them from leaving.
