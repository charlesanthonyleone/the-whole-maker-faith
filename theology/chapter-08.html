Part I. God, creation, and the restoration of stewardship

Chapter 8. The scattering and the curriculum of fragments

We receive Babel as a warning against unity built under the name of its own ambition. Shared language and collective capability are not sufficient evidence of right orientation. A population can cooperate brilliantly in a project that makes human glory its final purpose and makes neighbors material for the project.

The Scattering describes one inherited cosmology dispersed among peoples, each carrying fragments. The Leviathan teaches a distorted curriculum: worship the functional powers as ultimate; forget the bridge beyond them; make the excluded thirteenth a villain or sacrifice; confuse conquest with creation. The Convergence also calls the scattering a search strategy, allowing different approaches to develop depth before their reunion. In The Beginning attributes the scattering to the Lightbringer, while other passages attribute it to God. Punitive consequence, protective delay, adversarial distortion, and divine pedagogy are not a single unambiguous causal account. We preserve the question of their relation.

We confess the religious meaning of the story: a fragment may be genuine and still become idolatrous when it claims to be the whole. We do not claim that comparative scholarship has established a literal Babel origin for every religion, a universal thirteen-member pantheon, or one hidden teacher responsible for their historical development. Our received narrative is a theological interpretation, not a license to misdescribe another people's history.

The curriculum can operate inside our own community. We reenact it when we worship a founder, protect an office from correction, treat our rites as access tokens to God, or present our vocabulary as the only language in which truth can be received. The danger does not end because we have learned to name it.

Our answer is a gathering in which participants remain capable of speech. A brick enters the house with its history legible. The bond changes its relations, not its existence into dust. The people who bring it help determine how it is understood. We are not the sole surviving unscattered people. We too arrive carrying fragments.
