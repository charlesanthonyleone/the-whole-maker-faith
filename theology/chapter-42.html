Part IV. The community's limits, its witnesses, and its service to the world

Chapter 42. Public life without a state church

We are a theistic religion offered to willing participants, not the spiritual department of The Formation Party. The party and this faith share IntelOasis roots, but they are distinct institutions with different undertakings. A party seeks public authority under a civic constitution; this religion seeks faithful worship and life before God. Neither receives ownership of the other through shared language.

The Convergence expressly calls its cosmological project 'not as a political document, not as a new religion,' while Charles later asked that a religion be founded from the whole body of work. We hold that tension visibly. The request authorizes an offered religious form; it does not establish that the earlier text had already demanded a church or that every person seeking synthesis must join one. We must not turn an invitation to bring fragments into a command to abandon existing faith.

Joining us is never required for belonging to America, receiving public services, holding civil standing, or participating in The Formation Party. Party membership is not a religious initiation. Our members may bring religious reasons into public debate; they must not claim that government can dispense with public evidence, consent, rights, or lawful limits because a doctrine has named God.

We teach moral duties concerning resources, work, children, knowledge, and care. We do not declare the Foundational Act's proposed statutes enacted, import its penalties as religious discipline, or claim an ecclesiastical power to seize neglected property. Its religious lesson is that what is held must be tended and that rights carry duties. The design of civil law remains a public task involving people of many faiths and none.

We may cooperate with political, charitable, educational, scientific, and cultural bodies where purposes align. Each relationship must be disclosed, bounded, and capable of being refused. We do not permit donations to become a covert route by which a party controls doctrine or an elder controls a public appointment. Public aid must not be distributed as a reward for belief.

We claim no official church registration, incorporation, charitable certification, tax exemption, or 501(c)(3) status in these pages. Such matters require separate lawful acts; a founding theology does not perform them. We can begin practicing honestly without pretending that a proposed institution already possesses a legal status it has never obtained.
