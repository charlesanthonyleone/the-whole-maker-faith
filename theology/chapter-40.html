Founding theology

Part IV. The community's limits, its witnesses, and its service to the world

Chapter 40. Elders as lamps and ministries as tasks

We recognize elders as lamps: people whose lives offer tested light and who remain capable of receiving new light from others. Recognition is testimony to what has been lived, not a grant of jurisdiction over another person's conscience. The elder who cannot learn from the young has ceased to illuminate the road ahead.

We establish no magisterium, priestly ranks, exclusive line of ordination, or office that holds truth back behind a title. We do not make the founder, the most eloquent speaker, the largest donor, or the keeper of the source archive the final interpreter of God. The Warning's claim that people designed to rule end up ruling remains testimony addressed to rulers and their responsibility; it does not prove that whoever has acquired religious influence was divinely selected to keep it.

A community nevertheless needs work done. We may appoint facilitators, readers, caretakers, record keepers, treasurers, teachers, and people responsible for safety. These are bounded administrative tasks, not sacred grades. Their duties, duration, competence, supervision, and removal must be clear before authority expands around them. No task confers an entitlement to intimate information beyond what it actually requires.

Recognition as an elder does not automatically qualify someone to investigate abuse, counsel a medical crisis, handle funds, or mediate a dangerous household. Competence is specific. Where a matter exceeds our ability, we seek qualified help rather than enlarge the elder's spiritual title until it appears to cover the gap.

Those entrusted with teaching must distinguish source, interpretation, evidence, and proposal. Those entrusted with resources must account for receipts, commitments, and actual use without exposing recipients' private lives. Those entrusted with safety must have a route for reporting concerns that does not depend on the person accused. No office is restricted by sex, ancestry, nationality, wealth, or a claimed spiritual bloodline.

An elder may lose recognition when their life contradicts its basis. Repair may be real without immediate restoration of title. A person who confesses abuse does not thereby become a more compelling guide for those they harmed. We honor change without asking the vulnerable to authenticate it by returning to their reach. The lamp serves the traveler; the traveler does not exist to preserve the lamp's reputation.
